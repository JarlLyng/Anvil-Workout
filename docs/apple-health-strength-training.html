Apple Health for Serious Lifters

Most Apple Health integrations in fitness apps are an afterthought: save something called "Functional Strength Training", call it a day. Iron Workout does it properly — writes real workout sessions, reads calories burned and heart rate from your Apple Watch, and respects Apple's strict privacy model at every step.

What gets written to Apple Health

When you finish a workout, Iron Workout writes a Traditional Strength Training workout session to Apple Health. The session includes:

  • Start and end time of the workout
  • Duration (excluding any time you paused)
  • Workout type — strength training, marked as indoor

The session shows up in the Health app alongside your runs, cycles, and any other logged activity. If you have third-party tools that read Health — trainer dashboards, nutrition apps, research studies — your lifting activity is now visible to them like any other workout.

What gets read from Apple Health

During a workout, if your Apple Watch is logging heart rate and active energy for the same time window, Iron Workout can surface those metrics on your session summary:

  • Active energy burned (calories) during the workout
  • Average heart rate across the session

Both numbers are pulled from Apple Health after the workout ends, and only for the exact time window of your session. They appear in your Iron Workout history view next to the workout you completed.

How Apple Watch fits in

Iron Workout does not have a watchOS companion app (yet). But because your Apple Watch is already logging heart rate and calories to Apple Health automatically, those numbers flow into Iron Workout via the Health integration without any watchOS code at all.

Practical setup:

  1. Wear your Apple Watch during your workout (no special app needed — your Watch is already tracking heart rate continuously).
  2. Optionally start an Apple Workout session on the Watch for more accurate calorie estimates. Any workout type works; Strength Training is closest.
  3. Run your workout in Iron Workout as normal.
  4. When you end the session, Iron Workout queries Health for heart rate and calories in that time window.

If you don't wear a Watch, Iron Workout still saves the workout — heart rate and calories just won't appear on that session.

Privacy and permissions

Apple's HealthKit is one of the strictest privacy frameworks on iOS. We think that's a good thing.

  • The first time you start a workout or tap "Grant Access" in Settings, iOS shows Apple's own permission dialog listing exactly what Iron Workout wants to read and write. You approve each category individually.
  • You can change permissions any time in Apple's Health app → your profile → Privacy → Apps and Services.
  • If you revoke write permission, Iron Workout stops saving workouts to Health. If you revoke read permission, we can't show calories and heart rate on session summaries.
  • Iron Workout never transmits Health data off your device. Apple's framework handles all access on-device; we simply ask for the data and display it.

A note on calorie estimates

Active energy burned during resistance training is notoriously hard to measure. Apple Watch estimates are based on heart rate plus movement, which captures the aerobic cost of a workout but underestimates the metabolic cost of heavy compound lifts. Treat the calorie number as directional — useful for tracking trends, not as a hard number to plan your nutrition around.

Why most workout apps skip this

HealthKit integration takes real work: permission flows, background fetches, workout builder APIs, correct unit handling, and respecting Apple's privacy boundaries. A lot of subscription-based fitness apps either skip it entirely or write a generic "Other" workout type that doesn't integrate with the rest of the Health ecosystem.

Iron Workout was built around HealthKit from the start. It's a local-first app, and Apple Health is the local-first fitness platform. The integration is natural.

What Iron Workout doesn't do (yet)

We're honest about the gaps:

  • No standalone Apple Watch app. You can't log sets from your wrist — yet. The Watch's role today is passive (heart rate, calories). A dedicated watchOS app is on the roadmap.
  • No HealthKit history read. We don't read past workouts from Health and pull them into Iron Workout's history. Our history view only shows sessions you logged with us directly.
  • No body weight or body measurements. Iron Workout focuses on lifting performance. If you track weight in the Health app, it stays there.
