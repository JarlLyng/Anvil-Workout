Pay Once. Own It Forever.

Most workout apps on the App Store are subscription-based. That works for some people — but it leaves a lot of lifters paying 50-100 € a year to track something they could just as well track on their own phone. Iron Workout exists as the one-time-purchase alternative for people who are done renting access to their own data.

What the subscription model gives you

To be fair: subscriptions pay for real things. They fund ongoing development, cloud infrastructure, social features, and the staff to keep it running. If you value what you get, subscriptions are a perfectly reasonable model.

Subscription-based workout trackers typically offer:

  • Cross-device sync (iPhone, iPad, web)
  • Social feed, friends, and sometimes leaderboards
  • Cloud backup independent of your device
  • Frequent content updates (new programs, exercises, challenges)
  • Recommendations and analytics powered by a server

Where it breaks down for lifters

After years of using subscription trackers, many lifters eventually notice the same pattern of friction:

Your data lives behind a paywall

The workout history you're logging is personal — years of squats, benches, and PRs. When you stop paying, that history often goes read-only or locks behind a "free tier" that strips out the actual analysis features. Export options exist, but they're usually buried or limited.

Account required, even to use offline

Subscription apps almost always require an account. You sign up, you sign in on each device, and your workout is gated by email verification or OAuth. If your phone loses signal in the gym basement and the app decides to re-authenticate, you're stuck.

Features you don't want, priced into the monthly fee

Social feeds, community challenges, global leaderboards, coaching marketplaces — these all cost money to run, and that cost is in your subscription whether you use them or not. Many lifters just want to log a workout and see their PRs.

If the company pivots, your data follows

Apps get acquired. Subscriptions get "sunset". Fitness startups have a notorious shutdown rate. When the lights go out, your years of workout history typically go with them — or into a CSV export you have to scramble to grab.

What Iron Workout does differently

Iron Workout is a paid app, one-time purchase, no recurring charges. You buy it on the App Store, you own it, and all future updates are included.

Beyond the pricing model, the whole app is designed around the idea that your data should outlive any single company:

  • Local-first storage. Your workouts live in SwiftData on your iPhone — not on a server we control.
  • No account required. Install the app and start training. No email, no password, no OAuth dance.
  • Export your full history as CSV any time from Settings — not as a retention tactic, but as a default capability.
  • iCloud backup comes free with your iPhone. Your Iron Workout data is included in your regular device backup, so restoring to a new phone is the same one-step restore as any other app.
  • If we ever stop shipping updates, the app on your device keeps working until iOS breaks compatibility — which is typically many years.
