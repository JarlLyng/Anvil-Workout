Questions.

Does Anvil Workout need a subscription?

No. It's a one-time purchase, with no subscription and no in-app purchases. Every feature is included on iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch, and updates come with it.

Does it work without a signal?

Yes. Planning, running and reviewing a workout need no internet connection. Your programs and history are stored on your device and never uploaded. The only thing the app can send is a report if it crashes, hangs or hits an error. Reports never include your workouts, notes or Health data, and you can turn them off in Settings.

Does it work with Apple Health?

Yes, if you allow it. Each finished workout is saved to Apple Health, and Anvil reads back the calories and average heart rate Health recorded during it. Those numbers come from another source, usually an Apple Watch, so without one they are often empty.

Does the Apple Watch app work on its own?

No. Start the workout on your iPhone and the Watch follows it, so you can log and skip sets from your wrist. It needs no internet, but it does need your iPhone nearby.

Does my data sync between iPhone and iPad?

No. Each device keeps its own programs and history, and nothing syncs between them.

What happens to my data on a new iPhone?

It moves with your device backup. If you use iCloud Backup or back up to a computer, your Anvil data comes back when you restore to the new phone. Without a backup it stays on the old one.

Can I bring my history from Strong or Hevy?

Yes. Export a CSV from either app and import it in Settings, or the first time you open Anvil. The preview lists anything that can't come across, such as timed or distance sets, before anything is saved. Anvil can't import its own CSV export, so the export is a copy of your history to keep, not a backup to restore from.

Can my family use it?

Yes. Anvil Workout supports Family Sharing, so up to six family members can use it with Family Sharing turned on.

What can I log?

Programs with exercises, sets, reps, weight, rest and supersets. In the gym, each set's weight and reps, its type (working, warm-up, drop or failure) and an RPE if you want one. Afterwards, your history, personal records for weight and reps, an estimated one-rep max per lift, and volume, workouts and sets per week. Runs, planks and carries can't be logged, because a set is reps and weight.
