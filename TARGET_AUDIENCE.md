# Target Audience — Anvil Workout

Who Anvil Workout is for, why they buy, and how to reach them. This document
defines the ideal customer profile (ICP) so product, marketing, ASO, and copy
all point at the same person. It is also a template for the wider IAMJARL
portfolio strategy (see [Portfolio note](#portfolio-note-iamjarl)).

**Maintained by:** the team. Keep it consistent with `SEO_STRATEGY.md` and the
marketing site in `docs/`.

---

## TL;DR

Anvil Workout is for the **self-coached intermediate-to-advanced lifter in the
Apple ecosystem who is tired of renting software.** They already know how to
train, they log to drive progressive overload, and they would rather pay once
and own a fast, private, native app than rent a social feed they don't use.

We do **not** chase the mass market that Hevy (12M+ users, social, freemium) and
Fitbod (AI coaching, subscription) own. We win a focused, high-intent slice of it:
the lifter who values **ownership, privacy, speed, and focus** over community and
coaching. That slice is small in share but high in willingness to pay, low in
support burden, and loud in word-of-mouth.

---

## The market we play in

- **Strength training is booming.** Strava logged 500M+ strength-activity uploads
  in 2025, one of its fastest-growing categories. Logging is mainstream now.
- **The category is subscription-dominated.** Hevy (12M+ users) grew by offering a
  generous free tier with a built-in social feed; Strong (3M+ users) is the
  precision-logging incumbent that moved features behind a paywall; Fitbod sells
  AI-generated workouts on subscription. Every major player monetizes recurring.
- **Subscription fatigue is real and rising (2025-2026).** Consumers increasingly
  resent stacking monthly charges and prefer to *own* tools — they want "closure,"
  and paying once feels decisive. One-time purchases thrive specifically for
  utilities and tools people depend on and want to keep working.
- **Apple users pay.** iPhone owners earn ~43% more on average than Android users
  and are conditioned to pay for apps; iOS dominates premium markets (US, UK,
  Nordics, Germany, Australia, Japan). Our entire addressable base lives here.
- **Privacy is a live concern.** A Duke study found 79% of popular health/fitness
  apps share data with third parties, and only 28% of users knew. On-device,
  offline-first apps are a growing, explicitly-demanded niche, with regulatory
  tailwind (e.g. HIPRA introduced late 2025).

**Strategic read:** the big players optimize for scale, social, and recurring
revenue. That leaves a deliberately under-served seam — lifters who want a focused
logging tool they own outright, with their data on their device. Anvil is built
exactly for that seam.

## Primary persona — "The self-coached lifter who owns their training"

> **Marcus, 31.** Software developer, lifts 4x/week. Runs his own 5/3/1 block,
> has an iPhone and Apple Watch, and has logged every workout for three years. He
> cancelled three subscriptions last year on principle. He doesn't want a feed,
> a coach, or a leaderboard — he wants to open the app, log the set, and see if
> he's progressing. He'll happily pay once for something that respects his time
> and his data.

- **Training level:** intermediate to advanced. Knows progressive overload, RPE,
  deloads, supersets. Does **not** need prescribed programming or AI coaching.
- **Behavior:** logs consistently (the behavior most correlated with results),
  reviews PRs and 1RM trends, runs a known program (Starting Strength, StrongLifts,
  5/3/1, GZCLP, PPL) or his own.
- **Tech:** deep in the Apple ecosystem — iPhone + Apple Watch, uses Apple Health,
  values native feel and speed over cross-platform breadth.
- **Money:** higher disposable income, already pays for apps, but is actively
  hostile to *recurring* charges for a tool he uses solo.
- **Values:** ownership, privacy, focus, longevity. Wants his years of history to
  outlive any single company.

**What he's hiring Anvil to do (jobs-to-be-done):**
1. Log sets fast in the gym without friction, fully offline.
2. Show whether he's actually progressing (PRs, volume, estimated 1RM).
3. Keep his entire training history his, forever, with no account and no cloud.
4. Stop paying monthly for something he uses alone.

## Secondary personas

### 1. The subscription escapee (highest-intent acquisition target)
Currently on Strong or Hevy, frustrated by a paywall or a feature going behind
subscription, or just done with the monthly charge. **The CSV import from Strong
and Hevy removes their only real switching cost** — years of history. This is the
single strongest acquisition wedge; lead with it.

### 2. The privacy-first minimalist
Cares about data ownership, may be de-Googled or running a minimal phone setup.
Chooses tools by "where does my data live?" Anvil's no-account, 100%-offline,
on-device storage is the headline feature, not a footnote. Reachable in privacy
and digital-minimalism communities, not just fitness ones.

### 3. The Apple-ecosystem loyalist
Buys native-first, wants the Apple Watch companion, the Live Activity, the
home-screen widget, HealthKit sync. Picks the app that feels like it belongs on
iOS. Values polish and "no account required" as a feature.

## Anti-personas — who Anvil is *not* for

Naming these keeps the product focused and the copy honest.

- **The beginner who wants to be told what to do.** They need guided/AI programming
  (Fitbod, a coach). Anvil assumes you bring your own plan.
- **The social exerciser.** They want a feed, friends, kudos, challenges, and
  community programs. That's Hevy's core; Anvil deliberately has none of it.
- **The multi-platform / Android user.** Anvil is Apple-only by design. No web app,
  no Android, no cross-platform cloud sync.
- **The strictly-free user.** Someone who will never pay for an app is not our
  customer, and trying to convert them dilutes the product. Anvil is a paid,
  one-time purchase and stays that way.

## Where to reach them

- **Fitness communities (high intent):** r/Fitness, r/weightroom, r/naturalbodybuilding,
  r/StartingStrength, r/531Discussion, lifting Discords. These users log and
  discuss tools constantly. Migration-from-subscription and "own your data" angles
  land here.
- **Privacy / minimalism communities:** r/privacy, r/degoogle, r/digitalminimalism,
  privacy-focused newsletters. Lead with offline-first and no-account.
- **Apple-focused channels:** App Store search/ASO, Apple-centric blogs and
  newsletters, "best workout apps for Apple Watch" roundups (a recurring search).
- **Maker / indie channels:** Indie Hackers, Product Hunt, Hacker News — receptive
  to the one-time-purchase, anti-subscription, indie-maker story (already in play).
- **ASO keyword themes:** "offline workout tracker," "no subscription gym log,"
  "private workout app," "Apple Watch strength tracker," "import from Strong/Hevy,"
  "one-time purchase workout app."

## Messaging that resonates (and what to avoid)

**Lead with:**
- **Own it, don't rent it.** One-time purchase, no subscription, no account.
- **Your data stays yours.** 100% offline, on your device, no cloud, no tracking.
- **Bring your history with you.** Import from Strong or Hevy in one step.
- **Focused, not noisy.** No feed, no upsells, nothing between you and the next set.
- **Native and fast.** iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch, HealthKit.

**Avoid:**
- Calling the app "free" anywhere (it is a paid one-time purchase — see CLAUDE.md).
- Competing on social features, community size, or AI coaching — that's the
  competitors' turf and off-strategy.
- Over-promising cross-platform or cloud sync — it's intentionally Apple-only and
  local-first.

**Objections and answers:**
| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "Why pay when Hevy/Strong have free tiers?" | You pay once and own it forever, with no feature held hostage and no data mining. |
| "What if you stop updating it?" | It keeps working on your device; your data is local and exportable. Updates are included, not gated. |
| "No sync to my other devices?" | Local Apple Watch sync and iCloud restore for a new iPhone/iPad. No account, by design. |
| "No social/community?" | That's the point. Anvil is for training, not posting. |

## What this means for the product

- **Protect the focus.** Every feature request gets tested against the primary
  persona. Social, feeds, and cloud accounts are off-strategy by default.
- **Double down on the moat:** offline-first, no account, data ownership, native
  Apple polish, one-time price. These are the reasons to choose Anvil over a bigger app.
- **Migration is the funnel.** Keep Strong/Hevy import excellent and visible — it
  converts the highest-intent segment. (Verify against real exports before promoting.)
- **Serve the advanced lifter's flexibility.** They reject rigid programming; keep
  logging fast and templates freeform (tags, supersets, RPE-friendly).
- **Lean into Apple surfaces** the competition under-serves: Apple Watch, Live
  Activity, widgets, HealthKit. These are buying reasons for the ecosystem loyalist.
